1. Essential notes

Polynomial foundations

Recall: Degree 0 = constant (horizontal line); degree 1 = linear (straight line); degree 2 = quadratic (parabola).

f(x) = aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ··· + a₂x² + a₁x + a₀,   aₙ ≠ 0

A polynomial of degree n is a sum of monomial terms. The degree n is a nonnegative integer.

Term (monomial): an individual summand, such as a₂x².
Standard form: terms in descending powers.
Coefficients: constants multiplying the powers of x.
Leading coefficient: aₙ.
Leading term: aₙxⁿ.

Zeros and turning points

A degree-n polynomial has at most n distinct real zeros and at most n − 1 local extrema.

To find zeros, solve f(x) = 0. The graph's x-intercepts are (zero, 0).

End behavior

As |x| → ∞, the leading term determines how the graph behaves.

DegreeLeading coefficientLeft endRight end
EvenPositive↑↑
EvenNegative↓↓
OddPositive↓↑
OddNegative↑↓

Multiplicity

If (x − r) appears m times, r has multiplicity m.

Even multiplicity: touches the x-axis and turns. Odd multiplicity: crosses the x-axis. Higher multiplicity creates more flattening.

Example: (x − 2)³(x + 1)² crosses at 2 and touches at −1.

Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

Every polynomial is continuous. If f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, there is at least one zero between a and b.

A sign change guarantees a zero; a zero of even multiplicity need not produce a sign change.

2. Complete concept notes

These sections follow the sequence of the four-page class handout. Open each concept for the rule, why it works, and a concrete example.

Degree and graph shapes

Degree 0: constant / horizontal line. Degree 1: linear / straight line. Degree 2: quadratic / parabola.

Degree 3 (cubic): opposite end directions, at most 3 real zeros and 2 local extrema. Degree 4 (quartic): matching end directions, at most 4 real zeros and 3 local extrema.

General theorem: degree n → at most n distinct real zeros and n − 1 local extrema. These are upper bounds, not guaranteed counts.

Leading-term test

For large |x|, the highest-degree term dominates the lower-degree terms. To predict end behavior, keep only the leading term.

f(x) = −x⁴ + x³ + 3x² − 2x + 7
behaves like −x⁴ as |x| → ∞

Therefore both ends fall: f(x) → −∞ as x → ±∞.

Zeros, intercepts and multiplicity

A zero r solves f(r) = 0, producing an x-intercept (r, 0). To find the y-intercept, compute f(0).

If (x − r)m is a factor, r has multiplicity m. Even m: touches and turns. Odd m: crosses. Higher multiplicity: flatter near the zero.

(x − 2)³(x + 1)²

At −1: multiplicity 2, touch. At 2: multiplicity 3, cross with flattening.

Intermediate Value Theorem

If f is continuous on [a, b], it takes every value between f(a) and f(b). Polynomials are continuous everywhere.

If f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, at least one real zero lies in (a, b).

Important: a zero need not cause a sign change: f(x) = x² touches zero at x = 0.

Synthetic division procedure

  1. Write all coefficients, including zero placeholders for missing powers.
  2. Place the candidate zero r on the left.
  3. Bring down the first coefficient; multiply by r; add; repeat.
  4. A remainder of zero confirms (x − r) is a factor.

For #52, use r = 2 on coefficients 1, −4, −44, 96 to obtain 1, −2, −48 and remainder 0.

Build a polynomial from zeros

  1. Convert each zero r to the factor (x − r).
  2. Multiply the factors.
  3. If monic, the leading coefficient must be 1.
  4. Check each original zero by substitution.

Zeros −2, 3, −5 → (x + 2)(x − 3)(x + 5) = x³ + 4x² − 11x − 30.

8. One-minute review

Before the test, can you do these without notes?

  1. State the meaning of degree, leading term, and leading coefficient.
  2. Use degree and leading sign to sketch both ends of a graph.
  3. Factor and find zeros, y-intercept, and multiplicities.
  4. Explain when a graph crosses or touches the x-axis.
  5. Locate or estimate extrema, and use the IVT correctly.
  6. Perform synthetic division and build a polynomial from zeros.

Graphing routine: standard form → degree and leading coefficient → end behavior → zeros/multiplicities → y-intercept → extrema → sketch.
